DENTITRACE · LEGAL INFORMATION

Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 13, 2026

This Privacy Policy (the "Policy") explains how DentiTrace (the "Service"), operated by Franklin Rodriguez Acosta (the "Operator"), processes personal data in connection with the Service. The Service is a multi-tenant clinical and administrative workspace used by dental clinics and authorized dental professionals. The configured jurisdiction is Ecuador.

1. Operator and contact details

The Service is operated by Franklin Rodriguez Acosta. The contact channel used for any privacy or data-protection request is described in Section 16. Additional identifying or address information required by applicable law should be displayed through the Service's legal configuration or legal notice without exposing private values in source-controlled documentation.

2. Who determines how data is processed

For patient and clinical information entered into the Service by a Clinic, the Clinic generally determines why the information is processed, which information is entered, who may access it, and how it is used in the professional relationship with the Patient. In that context, the Clinic generally acts as the party responsible for the processing of Patient information, and the Operator provides the technical Service on the Clinic's behalf.

For account administration, Service security, billing administration, support operations, and the Operator's own legal obligations, the Operator may determine the relevant purposes and means of processing and therefore may have an independent privacy role under applicable law.

The precise legal characterization may vary by jurisdiction, applicable law, and any data-processing agreement entered into with a Clinic.

3. Categories of personal data

Depending on how the Service is used, the Service may process the categories described below.

Account and authentication data: name and email address, authentication identifiers, password hashes handled by the authentication system, session and token information, and security-event metadata.

Clinic, team, and professional data: clinic name and configuration, clinic branding, professional profile information, roles and permissions, and membership and invitation information.

Patient and clinical data: sensitive health-related information entered by a Clinic, including patient identifiers and demographic information, odontograms, clinical encounters, diagnoses, treatment plans, prescriptions, attachments and uploaded files, generated clinical documents, and other clinical-record content entered by the Clinic.

Operational and audit data: audit events, consent records, invitation records, export records, security-relevant activity records, and timestamps and request identifiers.

Billing and subscription data: subscription and plan identifiers, entitlement and add-on information, payment-provider customer or subscription identifiers, and refund status and transaction-related references. The Service does not store full payment-card information.

Telemetry: limited technical information may be processed to operate, secure, diagnose, and improve the reliability of the Service, such as request identifiers, timestamps, error events, and deployment or service-region information.

Analytics and tracking: the production Service uses Google Analytics 4 and Meta Pixel for analytics, audience measurement, and conversion-related reporting. Their activation is controlled through the Service's tracking configuration. These services may use cookies, pixels, local identifiers, or similar browser/device technologies and may receive information such as page views and navigation paths, referral information, browser or device information, timestamps and technical identifiers, and conversion-related events associated with use of the Service.

4. Purposes of processing

Personal data may be processed to:

  • create and secure user accounts;
  • authenticate users and manage sessions;
  • operate the Clinic's clinical and administrative workspace;
  • store and retrieve Patient records at the Clinic's direction;
  • generate documents and exports requested by Authorized Users;
  • enforce roles, permissions, and tenant isolation;
  • maintain audit and consent records;
  • manage subscriptions, plans, add-ons, and refunds;
  • provide support and troubleshoot technical issues;
  • protect the Service against fraud, abuse, unauthorized access, and security threats;
  • comply with applicable legal obligations;
  • perform analytics, audience measurement, and conversion reporting through Google Analytics 4 and Meta Pixel, subject to applicable consent and privacy requirements.

The applicable legal basis depends on the category of data, the purpose of processing, the Operator's role, the Clinic's role, and the law governing the processing.

Where consent is relied upon, it may be withdrawn subject to applicable law. Withdrawal does not retroactively invalidate processing that was lawful before withdrawal.

Administrative contact details may also be used for material change notices, Service continuity, planned shutdown notices and instructions then available for retaining information.

5. Sensitive health information

Patient and clinical data may constitute sensitive or specially protected personal data.

The Clinic is responsible for establishing the lawful basis for entering and using Patient information through the Service and for obtaining any consent or authorization required in the Clinic-Patient relationship.

The Operator applies technical and organizational safeguards appropriate to the sensitivity of the information, including tenant-scoped authorization and access controls.

6. Payment processing and Lemon Squeezy

Payments are currently processed by Lemon Squeezy.

Lemon Squeezy acts as Merchant of Record for transactions processed through its checkout and independently processes information required for payment, taxation, fraud prevention, refunds, chargebacks, and related transaction functions.

The Service receives limited payment and subscription information needed to manage access and entitlements but does not store full payment-card details.

Lemon Squeezy's applicable privacy and buyer terms are available at:

  • Privacy Policy: https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/privacy
  • Buyer Terms: https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/buyer-terms

The Operator does not control Lemon Squeezy's independent processing activities.

7. Other recipients and service providers

Personal data may be shared with service providers where reasonably necessary to operate the Service, including categories such as:

  • infrastructure and hosting providers;
  • object-storage or database providers;
  • email or notification providers;
  • security or malware-scanning services;
  • analytics and tracking providers, including Google Analytics 4 and Meta Pixel;
  • payment providers;
  • professional advisers where necessary;
  • competent authorities where disclosure is legally required.

Information about relevant service providers and, where applicable, their processing jurisdictions may be made available through the Service, legal documentation, or the privacy contact where required by applicable law or contract.

Service providers handling personal data on the Operator's behalf are subject to appropriate contractual, confidentiality, and security obligations as required by applicable law.

Google Analytics 4 and Meta Pixel operate under Google and Meta's respective terms and privacy practices. These providers may independently process information on their own systems when their technologies are used. The Operator accesses resulting analytics or conversion information through the providers' dashboards and does not represent that all underlying tracking information is stored inside DentiTrace's own application database.

8. International data transfers

Personal data may be processed in jurisdictions other than Ecuador where the Operator's service providers, payment providers, Google Analytics 4, or Meta Pixel operate.

Where applicable law requires safeguards for an international transfer, the Operator will use a transfer mechanism or contractual safeguard recognized by the applicable legal framework.

Information about relevant transfer arrangements may be requested through the privacy contact in Section 1.

9. Retention and deletion

Retention depends on the type of information, the purpose for which it is processed, Clinic instructions where applicable, contractual requirements, and applicable legal obligations.

In general:

  • account data is retained while the account is active and for any additional period reasonably required for security, disputes, or legal obligations;
  • Clinic and membership data is retained while needed to administer the Clinic's use of the Service and for any required post-termination period;
  • Patient and clinical data is retained according to the Clinic's instructions, the availability of the Service, and applicable record-retention obligations;
  • audit and security records are retained for periods reasonably necessary for accountability, fraud prevention, security, and incident investigation;
  • billing and transaction records are retained for applicable accounting, tax, payment, and legal requirements;
  • telemetry is retained only as long as reasonably necessary for its operational or security purpose.

A Clinic may request account closure and deletion of Customer Data through the privacy or general contact channels identified in this Policy. The Operator may verify the identity and authority of the requester before acting on a deletion request, particularly where sensitive Patient or clinical information is involved.

Verified deletion requests are processed without undue delay and are ordinarily completed within 30 days, to the extent deletion is technically possible and legally permitted. The Operator may complete a request sooner.

Deletion of an account does not necessarily mean that every copy of every record can be erased immediately. Limited information may be retained where reasonably necessary for backups and disaster recovery, security and fraud prevention, immutable or integrity-protecting audit records, billing and transaction records, legal preservation duties, dispute handling, or other mandatory legal obligations.

Patient and clinical records may also be subject to professional or legal retention duties applicable to the Clinic. Where the Clinic controls those records, the Operator will act on valid Clinic instructions to delete or return them to the extent permitted by applicable law and the Service's technical capabilities.

Where the Service provides deletion or export functionality, the timing and scope of deletion may therefore be affected by backups, immutable audit records, legal preservation requirements, and technical recovery processes.

Where an existing post-termination export mechanism is available, the Terms describe the ordinary period during which a Clinic may use that mechanism. Statutory access or portability rights remain governed by applicable law and are not limited by that contractual export period where the law provides otherwise.

In a planned permanent shutdown, the Operator will communicate the options and instructions then available to retain or retrieve information before the Service ends. Return, retention, deletion and any later access will follow valid Clinic instructions, existing capabilities and applicable law. This Policy does not promise a particular export format, third-party migration or new tool.

10. Data-subject rights

Depending on applicable law, individuals may have rights such as:

  • access to their personal data;
  • correction of inaccurate or incomplete data;
  • deletion where legally available;
  • restriction or suspension of processing in applicable circumstances;
  • objection to certain processing;
  • portability where the applicable legal requirements are met;
  • withdrawal of consent where processing is based on consent;

Requests may be submitted to dentitrace@studios216.com, or, if a separate privacy contact is configured, to that address.

Where a request concerns Patient or clinical information entered by a Clinic, the Clinic will generally be the primary point of contact because it controls the professional record and the purposes for which that information is used. The Operator will provide reasonable technical assistance to the Clinic where required and technically possible.

Identity verification may be required before a request is fulfilled, particularly where sensitive Patient or clinical data is involved.

Service shutdown does not extinguish individuals' applicable statutory rights or continuing obligations concerning their data.

The Operator uses technical and organizational measures designed to protect personal data against unauthorized access, alteration, disclosure, loss, or destruction.

Depending on the relevant Service component, measures may include:

  • tenant-scoped authorization;
  • role and permission controls;
  • encrypted transport;
  • authentication and session controls;
  • rate limiting;

12. Minors

The Service is not intended for minors to create unrestricted user accounts on their own behalf.

However, a Clinic may enter Patient records relating to minors as part of lawful dental care.

The Clinic is responsible for determining the lawful basis, consent or guardian authorization, and any heightened safeguards required for a minor's Patient record.

The Service may provide technical functionality to record consent or related information, but the Operator does not determine whether a particular clinical consent is legally or professionally sufficient.

The Service may provide AI-assisted features that process Customer Data solely to deliver functionality requested or enabled by the Clinic. Use of such a feature does not authorize the Operator or an external AI provider to use Customer Data for general-purpose model training or fine-tuning. 14. Cookies, analytics, and tracking technologies

The Service uses cookies, pixels, browser identifiers, and similar technologies in connection with analytics, audience measurement, and conversion reporting. The production Service uses Google Analytics 4, for analytics, usage measurement, and understanding how users interact with the Service, and Meta Pixel, for conversion measurement, audience-related reporting, and understanding whether actions associated with the Service result from or relate to Meta advertising or campaign activity. Activation of these services is controlled through the Service's tracking configuration.

Cookies are small data files or identifiers that websites and online services may store or read through a user's browser or device. Pixels and similar technologies can help measure whether a page was viewed, whether an action occurred, or how users interact with a website or application. Not every analytics or measurement interaction necessarily uses a cookie. Depending on the provider and configuration, these technologies may also use browser storage, device or session identifiers, request information, or similar mechanisms.

Depending on configuration, browser settings, user choices, and the provider's technology, Google Analytics 4 and Meta Pixel may receive information such as page or screen views, navigation paths, referral source information, browser and device characteristics, timestamps, IP-derived or similar technical information, cookie or browser identifiers, conversion or subscription-related events, and other technical events needed for analytics or measurement. The Service does not intentionally send Patient clinical content, diagnoses, treatment notes, prescriptions, attachments, or other clinical-record contents to Google Analytics 4 or Meta Pixel for analytics or advertising measurement.

Google Analytics 4 and Meta Pixel are third-party services operated by Google and Meta, respectively. The Operator generally reviews analytics and conversion information through the dashboards, reports, and tools provided by those services. The Operator does not need to store the providers' tracking cookies or their complete underlying analytics datasets in DentiTrace's own application database for these services to function. Information processed through these technologies may be handled on Google or Meta infrastructure and is subject to the providers' respective privacy practices.

The Service uses analytics and tracking technologies for purposes such as understanding general usage of the Service, measuring navigation and engagement, identifying technical or usability patterns, measuring subscription, registration, or conversion-related events, evaluating the effectiveness of marketing or advertising campaigns, and improving product and operational decisions using aggregated or reported analytics. These technologies are not used to make clinical decisions or to diagnose or treat Patients.

Where applicable law requires consent before non-essential analytics, advertising, or tracking technologies may be activated, the Service will use an appropriate consent or preference mechanism for affected users. Where such a mechanism is provided, a user may be able to accept, reject, or change preferences for non-essential tracking technologies. Browser settings, privacy controls, content blockers, and provider-specific controls may also affect how cookies or similar technologies operate. Rejecting non-essential analytics or advertising technologies should not prevent access to core clinical functionality, except where a particular non-essential feature inherently depends on the rejected technology.

Google and Meta provide their own privacy information and controls relating to their services. Their policies and controls are maintained by those providers and may change independently of DentiTrace. The Operator does not control how Google or Meta design their browser technologies, dashboards, or independent privacy controls.
